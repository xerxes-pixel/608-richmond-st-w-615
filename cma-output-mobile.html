Unit #613, at 500 sq ft with a smaller footprint and no parking but offset by a full 2024–2025 renovation and a downtown view, adjusts to $510,000. That kind of convergence from such different starting points is exactly the signal we want to see.

The broader market backs up a measured approach here. Toronto C01's 1-bed condo segment has been cooling steadily — average sale price has fallen from roughly $698,682 in 2023 to $524,433 today, inventory sits at 5.27 months, and August's sales-to-new-listings ratio of 39.4% tells us buyers currently have the leverage. Unit #1203, a brand-new listing at $809,000, illustrates the risk on the other side: a 1+den nearly 30% larger than the subject, priced well above where the comps support, with only one day on market to prove it. We are not the market, and neither is any one listing — the buyers are, and the adjusted sold data is what tells us what they've actually been willing to pay.

Given all of this, we recommend positioning 608 Richmond St W #615 in the $505,000–$520,000 range. It sits right where the strongest, most comparable evidence clusters, gives us room to generate competition rather than invite a standoff, and reflects the market we're actually selling into today — not the one from a few years ago.
